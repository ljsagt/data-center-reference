Watchlist

  • InP substrates and optical chips: Citi forecasts InP substrate prices could rise two to three times in 2027–28, while JX plans a seven-to-tenfold capacity increase. These are forecasts and future supply. InP substrates, TIA/driver chips and photodetectors have no scored rows; their price changes are not transferred to lasers or finished modules.
  • Optical connectors, fiber arrays and test equipment: Seikoh Giken’s inspection lead times extend from six to eight–nine months despite planned cleanroom expansion. Its connector capacity is rising and polishing-equipment prices are not being raised. Largan’s qualified fiber-array parts may broaden supply in 2027. These products are separate from finished CPO modules.
  • Liquid-cooling leak sensors: Tatsuta plans roughly 2.5 times end-2026 capacity by September 2027 and holds more than half of the reported water-leak-sensor market. Demand is strong, but the note does not identify leak-detection cables or the scored CDU sensor bundle, nor current allocation or ASP changes. Keep the opportunity and future relief here.
  • Silicon capacitors, thin-film inductors and HVDC racks: Nomura’s simulation shifts capacitance from output to input rails under vertical power/IVR. Silicon capacitors and thin-film inductors have no matching rows; they are not automatically TLVR inductors. MS expects broad HVDC adoption in late 2027, while Jefferies flags Nvidia timing delays. Whole-rack content gains are not device ASP increases.
  • Nearline HDD, heads and media: Edgewater says Resonac media is sold out through 2028 and new capacity takes about two years. Toshiba’s expansion still depends on external heads and media; Cowen and Evercore question the headline share gain. TDK head-sale reports are disputed and no completed sale is shown. No HDD, head or media row exists.
  • Generic DRAM, NAND and server CPUs: Nanya/Winbond and TrendForce notes point to further conventional-memory price increases. BNP’s large 2030 CPU forecast is long-term demand evidence. These are separate products from HBM and SOCAMM. Evercore’s broader 40-plus-week lead times also come with double-ordering and small-OEM credit warnings; generic shortages are not assigned to every component.
  • Foundry wafers and bonding equipment: Citi sees wafer pricing upside from 2027, while MS says existing LTAs through 2028 and spare expansion options limit major wafer ASP gains. UBS flags later hybrid-bonding adoption as customers prioritize available supply. Foundry wafers and bonding tools have no rows; the GFS interposer deal is future CoWoS supply-chain relief.
  • Complete cooling systems, BBU and turbine parts: AVC growth contrasts with Auras shipment delays attributed to timing and floods. GS sees strong integrated BBU/CBU positions; Bernstein forecasts more competition and lower BBU margins by 2032. These broad systems do not identify pump, valve, battery-cell or controller availability. Bernstein also flags turbine-part constraints without enough detail to rescore complete turbomachinery.
