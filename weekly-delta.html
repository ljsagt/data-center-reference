2026-10-04 refresh versus 2026-09-27 baseline · Singapore-time week of September 28–October 4

One Page Summary

  • No scores changed across 165 components. ABF, HBM and MLCC evidence reinforced scores already at the ceiling. Future supply and architecture changes did not alter current buckets.
  • ABF substrates: higher prices and a persistent supply gap are supported by several checks. SEMCO and Shinko factory plans are credible future relief.

  • Glass-core substrates: fresh notes place broad production in 2030 or later, conflicting with earlier qualification/ramp expectations. Manufacturing difficulty remains high, but current customer allocation is not demonstrated.
  • New watchlist focus: fiber lead times are roughly 50 weeks and export ASPs are higher. Fiber has no scored row.
  • Eight local HTML files contained 667 cards. After excluding 201 older cards, seven files contributed 466 in-window messages and 466 distinct Outlook targets. Repeated events and translated notes were grouped.
  • No new Third Bridge summary/export or validated /tmp/thirdbridge_clean_* directory was available. The latest August 15 package was already in the baseline. Research used local files only; some summaries are excerpts and forecasts remain uncertain.

Watchlist

  • Fiber, cable and connectors: Edgewater reports roughly 50-week fiber lead times. August export ASP rose 112% for fiber and 143% for cable, with mix affecting the figures. The English and Japanese GS notes are one release. Connector and cable-backplane content is rising, but this does not isolate Backplane PCB fabrication.
  • Optical policy and upstream parts: The proposed US-content rule for 3.2T modules is provisional and aimed at 2028–29. InP dependence, ceramic packages, pump lasers, OCS and NPO do not have matching rows. New laser architectures may broaden suppliers; no current policy-driven score change is assumed.
  • Generic DRAM, NAND, enterprise SSD and CPU: Micron reports stronger NAND pricing and enterprise-SSD demand, while Citi relays further NAND price forecasts. SIG reports 25–30-week server-CPU lead times; MS argues near-term Muse CPU capacity is adequate. These products are outside the HBM/SOCAMM rows, and the CPU views conflict.
  • Nearline HDD, heads and media: Toshiba’s headline plan to double HDD capacity contrasts with channel checks showing a much smaller 2027 increase and unchanged customer allocation. Heads/media relief is mainly expected in 2028 or later. Keep both the expansion and execution risk visible.
  • Advanced-node foundry and packaging routes: JPM expects N3 shortages to widen and forecasts 2027 foundry price increases. EMIB-T substrate yield remains low despite better package yield. Foundry wafers and separate EMIB-T/CoPoS platforms have no standalone scored rows; their forecasts cannot be copied into CoWoS pricing.
  • Complete CDU supply and fan bearings: LG’s 2.5MW CDU is reported as Nvidia-qualified, widening complete-system options. Minebea ball-bearing utilization is 90–95%. Neither source isolates availability or pricing of the scored pumps, valves, heat exchangers or turbine bearings.
  • Passive-component alliance and 800V power: TDK and Taiyo Yuden signed a non-binding MLCC/inductor MOU; repeated broker coverage is one event. There is no completed capital tie-up or proven pricing effect. OCP’s 800V DC focus and Renesas PMIC capacity plans remain roadmap evidence.
  • On-site power and permitting: Project Jupiter/Bloom permit approval is expected with some timing delay. Planned power projects still face execution risk. One local UBS summary has conflicting GW figures, so exact capacity numbers are omitted. Site-level evidence does not identify a scored generator or transformer part.
